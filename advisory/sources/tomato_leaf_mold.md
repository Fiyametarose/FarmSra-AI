# Tomato Leaf Mold

## Symptoms and spread
Pale green or yellow patches appear on upper leaf surfaces, with olive-green to gray-brown velvety growth below. The fungus persists on crop residue and spreads by spores in humid greenhouses, tunnels and dense plantings.

## Prevention and cultural control
Use drip irrigation, keep foliage dry, increase spacing and airflow, ventilate protected structures, remove infected residue, and clean stakes and trellises. Resistant varieties can help, but resistance varies among pathogen races.

## Biological and chemical considerations
The source emphasizes humidity reduction and sanitation; it does not substantiate a biological cure. Fungicides may be considered preventively when cultural measures are insufficient, but they do not restore damaged leaves. Local authorization is unverified: consult extension services and follow only an officially registered tomato label in Kenya or Ethiopia.

## Monitoring and expert assessment
Check lower and inner leaves where humidity remains high. Request expert assessment if disease continues after ventilation and irrigation changes.
