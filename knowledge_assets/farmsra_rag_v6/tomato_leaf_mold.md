# Tomato leaf mold

## What this is [what]
Tomato leaf mold is caused by Passalora fulva (also called Fulvia fulva) and is favored by humid foliage.

## Signs to check [signs]
Pale yellow spots on the upper leaf surface often align with olive-green or brown velvety fungal growth underneath; older leaves may dry and die.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Examine the undersides: the characteristic velvet-like growth helps separate leaf mold from ordinary yellowing and other leaf spots.

## What to do now [action]
Lift and inspect both sides of multiple symptomatic leaves and check air circulation.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Lower sustained humidity, improve ventilation and spacing, avoid wetting leaves and use a locally suitable resistant variety if available.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Fungicides may be considered in some situations, but verify local registrations, likely benefit and appropriate professional guidance first.

## Weather and spread [weather]
High humidity, including in dense or protected cropping systems, is an important risk factor.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Check whether new upper leaves develop matching spots and obtain expert review if extensive foliage is affected.
