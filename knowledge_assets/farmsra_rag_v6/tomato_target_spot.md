# Tomato target spot

## What this is [what]
Target spot of tomato is caused by Corynespora cassiicola, a fungus that may affect leaves, stems and fruit.

## Signs to check [signs]
Small lesions can enlarge into light-brown spots with dark concentric rings and yellow halos; fruit may develop sunken brown spots.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Target spot resembles early blight and bacterial spot, especially early in development, so a single leaf image is not definitive.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect the inside and lower part of the canopy and photograph leaf spots and any fruit lesions.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Use clean transplants, reduce weed hosts and crop debris, improve airflow and consider rotation to a nonhost crop when feasible.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
A locally registered fungicide may be used after disease confirmation and expert evaluation; effectiveness and resistance patterns vary, so do not prescribe brands or schedules.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Extended leaf wetness and high humidity can favor infection; wind and splash can distribute fungal spores.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Check for rapid leaf loss or worsening fruit spots and request expert input when the cause is uncertain.
