# Tomato: two-spotted spider mite (pest; outside disease scope)

## What this is [what]
Two-spotted spider mites are tiny arthropod pests and are not a tomato fungal, bacterial or viral disease. This class is present in the model but pest-treatment decisions are outside the initial disease advisory scope.

## Signs to check [signs]
Fine pale stippling or yellowing, sometimes with thin webbing, may occur on leaves. Tiny moving mites can be seen beneath affected leaves with magnification.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Look for mites or webbing: dry-weather stress and nutrient symptoms may cause yellowing without mites.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect the undersides of several leaves and take close-up photos of any visible mites or webbing.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Reduce severe water stress where possible and conserve natural mite predators; broad-spectrum insecticides can worsen mite problems by reducing natural enemies.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Do not issue an automated miticide prescription for this out-of-scope class. A pest specialist can discuss suitable biological or locally registered controls.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Hot, dry conditions can support rapid mite increase; weather alone does not prove an infestation.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
If mites are seen and damage spreads, offer a farmer-initiated expert referral and clearly label the model scope limitation.
