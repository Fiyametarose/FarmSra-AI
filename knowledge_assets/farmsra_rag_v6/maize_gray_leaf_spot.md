# Maize gray leaf spot

## What this is [what]
Gray leaf spot is a fungal maize leaf disease associated with Cercospora species.

## Signs to check [signs]
Long, narrow, rectangular tan-to-gray lesions are often bounded by leaf veins, initially appearing as smaller discolored spots.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
These flat rectangular lesions differ from common-rust pustules and from the larger elliptical or cigar-shaped lesions of northern leaf blight.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect lower and upper canopy leaves; photograph lesion shape and check whether many plants show similar patterns.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Use resistant varieties, appropriate crop rotation and residue management where compatible with local soil-conservation needs.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
A suitable registered foliar fungicide may be considered if disease threatens yield; necessity depends on stage, severity, field conditions and local expert guidance.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Warm, humid conditions and prolonged leaf moisture can favor infection. Infected maize residue can carry the pathogen between crops.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Record the affected area and progression; seek help when lesions spread rapidly or are difficult to distinguish.
