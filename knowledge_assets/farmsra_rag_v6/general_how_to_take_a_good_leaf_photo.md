# Taking crop-leaf photos for diagnosis

## What this is [what]
Clear photographs help record symptoms, but a photo cannot replace field inspection.

## Signs to check [signs]
Take a close-up of the affected leaf, both upper and lower surfaces, and an additional view of the entire plant and nearby plants.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Blurring, shadows, color filters and photographs of the wrong crop can make symptoms difficult to interpret.

## What to do now [action]
Use natural light without harsh glare; keep the leaf in focus. Capture the lesion and an overall view, and record crop type, location and symptom history.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Keep earlier photos if the problem changes over time. Do not reuse images of a different plant as if they came from the current case.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Do not select any plant treatment based on image quality alone.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Record recent rain, dry spells or other environmental changes only as context.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
If key symptoms are on stems, fruit, tubers or roots, photograph those too and seek field evaluation when necessary.
