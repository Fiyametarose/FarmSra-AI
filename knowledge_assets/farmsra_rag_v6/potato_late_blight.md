# Potato late blight

## What this is [what]
Late blight of potato is caused by the water mold Phytophthora infestans; it can damage foliage and tubers.

## Signs to check [signs]
Irregular green to brown water-soaked leaf areas enlarge rapidly; white growth may be seen at lesion margins in very humid conditions. Stems may darken and tubers may develop firm brown decay.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Do not confuse with early blight’s smaller spots and distinct concentric rings. Similar symptoms may require field or laboratory confirmation.

## What to do now [action]
Promptly check multiple plants and tubers; avoid moving suspect infected material to other growing areas. Contact an expert quickly if the pattern matches rapid late blight.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Use clean certified seed, remove volunteer potato plants and unmanaged tuber waste, avoid prolonged wet foliage where possible and use locally appropriate preventive measures.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Late blight can require timely crop-specific protectant strategies. Select no product without confirming the diagnosis and local registration; previously infected tissue is not restored by fungicides.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Cool to mild, wet or very humid weather can support rapid spread. Spores can move to other potato and tomato plants.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Inspect rapidly changing patches and nearby potatoes and tomatoes; seek timely professional advice because losses can be substantial.
