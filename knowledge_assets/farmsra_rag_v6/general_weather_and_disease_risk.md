# Weather and crop-health context

## What this is [what]
Rain, humidity, temperature and leaf wetness can influence infections, but the effect depends on the crop and pathogen.

## Signs to check [signs]
Consider weather together with actual lesions, pest signs, crop stage, farm practices and field observations.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Rainfall or high humidity alone cannot confirm a specific disease; an absence of rain cannot rule one out.

## What to do now [action]
Use weather observations for the diagnosis-case location, with a timestamp and clear distinction between observed weather and a forecast.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Where disease-specific evidence supports it, consider ventilation, canopy drying and reduced leaf wetness while avoiding damage from excessive pruning or water stress.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Do not produce pesticide thresholds or application schedules from general weather data.

## Weather and spread [weather]
For example, prolonged moisture can favor late blight, while hot dry conditions can favor mite buildup; these are risk clues, not proof.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Recheck symptoms after weather changes and indicate if the weather source is missing, stale or for a different location.
