# Tomato Septoria leaf spot

## What this is [what]
Septoria leaf spot of tomato is caused by the fungus Septoria lycopersici.

## Signs to check [signs]
Many small, usually round gray-centered spots with dark margins appear especially on lower leaves; tiny black fruiting bodies may be visible in spots.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Early blight more often forms larger ringed lesions; bacterial spot and other leaf damage can still resemble Septoria.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect lower leaves and look for small dark dots inside pale spot centers; photograph leaf undersides and the whole plant.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Improve airflow and spacing, water near the base, use mulch to reduce soil splash and remove infected residues as appropriate.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Where disease severity justifies protection, a crop-registered fungicide may help reduce additional infection; follow label and local expert advice, not invented application rates.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Wet leaves and splash can aid disease development and spread.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Track yellowing and loss of lower leaves; consult an expert if most of the canopy becomes affected.
