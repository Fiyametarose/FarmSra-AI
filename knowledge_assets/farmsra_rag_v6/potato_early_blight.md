# Potato early blight

## What this is [what]
Early blight of potato is commonly associated with the fungus Alternaria solani.

## Signs to check [signs]
Older potato leaves develop dark brown spots with concentric target-like rings; surrounding leaves may yellow. Some infected tubers show dry, sunken lesions.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Late blight more often shows rapidly expanding irregular water-soaked lesions, sometimes with white growth during humid weather.

## What to do now [action]
Check older and newer leaves and inspect tubers if affected. Photograph characteristic rings and note how quickly symptoms appeared.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Maintain balanced nutrition and irrigation to limit plant stress, use healthy planting material and follow appropriate crop hygiene and rotation.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Crop-specific registered fungicides may be relevant when early disease threatens yield; an expert should evaluate need and local label requirements.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Leaf moisture and favorable temperatures can aid infection. Older or stressed foliage is often affected first.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Record spread through the canopy and seek help if rapid blighting or tuber damage occurs.
