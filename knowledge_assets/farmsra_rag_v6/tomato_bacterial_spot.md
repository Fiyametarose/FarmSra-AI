# Tomato bacterial spot

## What this is [what]
Bacterial spot of tomato is caused by Xanthomonas bacteria, not by a fungus.

## Signs to check [signs]
Dark, sometimes water-soaked leaf spots can occur with yellowing; immature fruit may develop small raised, rough or scabby lesions.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Small lesions can resemble fungal target spot or other bacterial diseases; a leaf photograph alone may not establish the cause.

## What to do now [action]
Check leaves and fruit on more than one plant. Use clean hands/tools and avoid handling plants while the foliage is wet.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Use clean seed and healthy transplants, reduce splash and overhead irrigation where possible, improve air movement and manage diseased residue.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Copper-based disease-management products may have variable usefulness and do not cure already damaged tissue; verify a suitable locally registered product and consult an expert. Do not assume a fungicide for early blight controls this bacterial disease.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Warm, wet conditions and splashing water can help bacteria spread.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Record newly appearing spots and fruit lesions; seek an agricultural professional if disease is worsening or similar diseases cannot be distinguished.
