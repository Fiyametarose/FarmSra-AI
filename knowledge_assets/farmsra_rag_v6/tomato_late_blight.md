# Tomato late blight

## What this is [what]
Late blight of tomato is caused by the water mold Phytophthora infestans and can progress quickly.

## Signs to check [signs]
Large irregular water-soaked patches turn brown; white growth may appear at lesion edges in humid conditions. Dark stem lesions or firm brown fruit lesions can occur.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Differentiate from the smaller ringed lesions of early blight. Similar damage can also result from noninfectious stresses.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect multiple plants immediately, document leaves, stems and fruit, and consult a qualified expert promptly if rapid late blight is suspected.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Use disease-free transplants, limit extended leaf wetness, manage volunteer tomato/potato plants and remove confirmed infected material safely.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
A registered preventive late-blight management program may be necessary in some crops; do not claim a fungicide cures existing dead tissue or specify products/rates without local expert checks.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Damp conditions and moderate temperatures favor late blight, and spores may disperse through air and water.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Check whether symptoms spread quickly to adjacent tomato or potato plants; escalate to an expert only when the farmer requests it.
