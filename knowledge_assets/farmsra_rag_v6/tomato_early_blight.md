# Tomato early blight

## What this is [what]
Early blight of tomato is commonly caused by Alternaria fungi and affects leaves, stems and sometimes fruit.

## Signs to check [signs]
Brown or black spots, often on older leaves first, may develop visible concentric target-like rings; affected leaves yellow and fruit can show dark sunken lesions.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Distinguish from Septoria’s numerous smaller pale-centered spots and late blight’s rapidly enlarging irregular water-soaked areas.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect older leaves and nearby plants; note whether leaves are wet and photograph rings and affected fruit.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Reduce prolonged leaf wetness and soil splash, support canopy airflow, remove infected debris and rotate away from related host crops where practical.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Fungicide use may be appropriate when a confirmed, significant disease threatens the crop. Only consider locally registered crop-specific products with professional advice.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Spores spread in wind and splashing rain; moisture supports infection, but weather cannot confirm early blight.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Track new lesions on upper leaves and seek help if blighting accelerates.
