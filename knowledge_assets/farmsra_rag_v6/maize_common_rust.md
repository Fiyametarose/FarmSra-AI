# Maize common rust

## What this is [what]
Common rust is a fungal disease of maize caused by Puccinia sorghi.

## Signs to check [signs]
Raised, powdery reddish-brown pustules form on maize leaves, sometimes on both surfaces. Older pustules may darken and the surrounding leaf may yellow.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Distinguish the raised spore-producing pustules from the flat rectangular lesions of gray leaf spot and the long oval lesions of northern leaf blight.

## What to do now [action]
Inspect several plants and both leaf sides. Record the share of foliage affected and take a close-up of any powdery pustules.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Choose locally adapted resistant maize varieties where available. Monitor crops and avoid making fungicide decisions from a single lesion or model output.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
Fungicides can be an option in some production systems, but they require local disease confirmation, an appropriately registered product and expert assessment of need; no chemical rate is provided.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Humidity can favor common rust. Spores can move with wind; weather by itself does not diagnose rust.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Observe progression, particularly on leaves important to crop growth; seek an agronomist if symptoms are extensive or atypical.
