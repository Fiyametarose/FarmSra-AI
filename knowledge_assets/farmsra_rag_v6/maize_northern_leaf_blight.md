# Maize northern leaf blight

## What this is [what]
Northern leaf blight is a fungal disease of maize associated with Setosphaeria turcica (Exserohilum turcicum).

## Signs to check [signs]
Long, oval or cigar-shaped gray-green to tan dead areas develop on leaves; severely affected leaves can become largely blighted.

## Conditions that may look similar [check]
Compared with gray leaf spot, lesions are typically broader and not strictly restricted between leaf veins. Resistant varieties may show smaller lesions.

## What to do now [action]
Examine multiple plants, including lower leaves, and photograph the characteristic long lesions.

## Management and prevention [manage]
Prefer suitable resistant varieties and rotation; manage infected maize residues where practical without compromising soil protection.

## Treatment considerations [treatment]
A locally registered fungicide may be considered only after assessing the crop stage, disease pressure and likely benefit with a local adviser.

## Weather and spread [weather]
Extended leaf wetness and moderate temperatures can favor disease; residues and airborne or splash-dispersed spores also matter.

## Monitoring and expert review [monitor]
Track progression into upper canopy leaves and obtain a field assessment if damage is extensive.
